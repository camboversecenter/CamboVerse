# Housewarming study — sources and interpretation

26 October 2026. User-requested traditional house-blessing update, following the wedding study.

## Reading record

- [ACCU Nara, 2005 training report](https://www.nara.accu.or.jp/img/report/grouptraining/2005.pdf), section 7, “The Ceremony for Entering a New House”, printed pp. 63–64. The search index supplied the text of these pages; direct PDF opening failed due to file size. The report was not read in full and its illustrations were not inspected. The selected regional account informs the chanting, central-post and optional-meal cards. It is not a national prescription.
- [Angkor Database review of *Cambodian Wooden Houses, 1,500 Years of Khmer Heritage*](https://angkordatabase.asia/books/cambodian-wooden-houses-1-500-years-of-khmer-heritage), Darryl L. Collins and Sokol Hok, SIPAR, 2022. Bibliography and review consulted; the complete 296-page book was not available. Do not imply direct reading of its pp. 155–156.

## Design decisions

The old generic marquee staging is replaced with an original, simplified raised timber house. The roof opens to reveal the interior. Stair, threshold, seating, water vessel, post and meal can be selected directly or through labelled buttons. The architecture is not a measured building reconstruction. It uses authored Three.js meshes and small procedural textures; it is not a Blender export.

Stable IDs `offerings`, `chanting` and `meal` remain, with `central-post` added. The sequence is a learning arrangement. Existing day metadata is retained for compatibility but should not be presented as a fixed historical timetable. The housewarming is distinguished from earlier construction rites; the old unsupported claim that it is almost never held inside a finished room was removed.

Object dimensions, food types/counts, furniture placement and materials are illustrative. The four seating places illustrate a regional example only. Sacred scripts, chants, officiant gestures and people have not been invented or simulated. Khmer text remains pending native review, and the template retains `needsReview: true`. Daylight supports inspection even where a source describes evening practice.

Normal retains the full lesson with fewer radial segments and no shadows. Ultra enables shadows and smoother objects. VR uses the same scene at Ultra with the origin on the raised floor. Real headset and low-end phone performance remain device-test tasks.

Review needs: a Khmer ceremony specialist should verify the represented practice, terminology, object arrangement and any future participants or narration before the scene is described as an authenticated reconstruction.
